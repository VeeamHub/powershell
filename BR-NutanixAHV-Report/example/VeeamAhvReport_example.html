How to read these numbers.

  • Median — the middle VM. Half of the VM backups finished in 7.7 min or less, half took longer.
  • Mean — the average (7.7 min). One very slow VM can pull it up, so on its own it can make things look better or worse than a typical run.
  • P90 — 9 out of 10 VM backups finished within 7.7 min. Only the slowest 10% took longer.
  • P95 — 19 out of 20 VM backups finished within 7.7 min. Only the slowest 5% took longer.
  • Max — the single slowest VM (7.7 min). A job cannot finish before its slowest VM does, however many workers you add.

Think of it like a queue at the checkout: the average tells you a typical wait, but P90 and P95 tell you how long the slow customers hold things up — and they decide when the shop can close. Plan the backup window on P90 rather than the average, so the slower VMs are allowed for.

Small sample: only 2 VM backup run(s) were measured. With this few, P90, P95 and Max are often the same run, so treat them as rough. Use -DaysBack to cover more runs.

Projection for one backup window. 2 VMs × mean 7.7 min ÷ 4 concurrent tasks ≈ 4 min (using P90: 4 min). The window can never be shorter than the slowest single VM, 8 min.

Formula: distinct VMs × per-VM duration ÷ concurrent tasks. It assumes every enabled worker can take any VM; if jobs are split across clusters, apply it per cluster using that cluster's task total.

Watch the gap between mean and P95 — a long tail means stragglers, and stragglers are what overrun a fixed window.
